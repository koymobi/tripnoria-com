How to Spend 5 Days in Lisbon and Sintra

A balanced route through Lisbon’s hills and riverfront, with one focused day among Sintra’s palaces.

By Tripnoria Editors · Published November 8, 2026

Five days gives Lisbon enough room to feel like a city rather than a sequence of viewpoints. The key is to group neighborhoods by terrain, protect one full day for Sintra and stop treating every hill as a shortcut. This itinerary favors morning exploration, slower lunches and evenings close to where the day ends. Reserve only the attractions that genuinely require it, verify current transport details and let the rest of the city unfold on foot.

Day 1: Baixa, Chiado and your first miradouro

Begin in the flatter streets of Baixa to understand the center before climbing. Walk through Rossio, continue toward the river at Praça do Comércio and turn west into Chiado for shops, cafés and book-lined streets. Choose one nearby viewpoint for late afternoon instead of racing between several. Lisbon’s paving can be slippery and the gradients are real, so supportive shoes matter more than squeezing every famous tram into the first day. Eat near your accommodation and keep the evening easy.

Day 2: Alfama without a rigid route

Enter Alfama early, using the cathedral or lower waterfront as a starting point, then climb gradually through lanes and small squares. Select one major historic site and allow the connecting streets to carry the rest of the morning. After lunch, descend by a different route toward the river. Tram 28 is iconic but often crowded; ride only if the queue and conditions make sense, and never let it determine the day. An evening of live fado can be meaningful when chosen for the music rather than as a compulsory dinner package.

Day 3: Belém and the western riverfront

Take public transport west and start with the monument or museum you value most. Belém’s major sights are close on a map but each deserves time, so choose two anchors and treat gardens, waterfront paths and a pastry stop as the connective tissue. Check entry arrangements before departure, especially in busy seasons. Return toward central Lisbon by late afternoon, then spend the evening in a neighborhood such as Príncipe Real or Estrela, where the pace feels more residential.

Day 4: one clear plan for Sintra

Leave early and decide your priority before boarding the train. Trying to combine every palace, the historic center and the coast usually produces more transfers than enjoyment. Pair one headline palace with one contrasting site or garden, allowing generous time for local buses, queues and steep approaches. Weather can differ from Lisbon, so carry a light layer even on a warm day. Buy timed tickets where required, verify official access information and return before exhaustion turns the final journey into the only thing you remember.

Day 5: markets, neighborhoods and a flexible finish

Use the final day for what the earlier days revealed. Explore Campo de Ourique, revisit Chiado, browse a market or follow the waterfront north from Cais do Sodré. This is also the right moment for a longer lunch or the museum you postponed. Keep plans within one or two adjacent areas and finish near a direct route back to your accommodation. A final viewpoint is more satisfying when you recognize the hills and river bends below it.

Small decisions that improve the itinerary

Stay near a useful metro or tram connection but expect to walk extensively. Load an offline map, carry water and avoid scheduling fixed reservations on opposite sides of the city. Watch belongings in crowded transport and use licensed taxis or recognized ride services when needed. Most importantly, leave a buffer around Sintra and arrival day. Lisbon is generous with detours, and the best five-day plan makes room for them rather than treating them as delays.
