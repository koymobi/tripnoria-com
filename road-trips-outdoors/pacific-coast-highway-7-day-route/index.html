Pacific Coast Highway: A 7-Day First-Timer Route

A north-to-south coastal drive with realistic distances, flexible stops and time to enjoy Big Sur.

By Tripnoria Editors · Published October 24, 2026

The Pacific Coast Highway is not a route to measure only in miles. Fog, road work, photo stops and the desire to sit above the ocean can turn a short segment into a full day—which is the point. This seven-day outline runs north to south so many coastal pullouts are easier to access, but current closures must determine the final route. Check official California road conditions before booking and again each morning; Highway 1 can change quickly after storms or landslides.

Day 1: San Francisco to Monterey

Leave the city after the busiest commute and follow the coast only where time and conditions make sense. The first day should be an orientation rather than a race through every beach town. Stop for one substantial walk or meal, then reach Monterey with daylight remaining. Use the evening for the waterfront or nearby Pacific Grove. Starting gently leaves energy for the more demanding curves and viewpoints ahead.

Day 2: Monterey, Carmel and Point Lobos

Keep the car mostly parked. Spend the morning around Monterey or the peninsula, then choose Carmel and a walk at Point Lobos if access and weather allow. Reserve entry or parking where required and follow posted trail rules. This short-distance day prevents the common mistake of entering Big Sur late, tired and already behind schedule. Refuel, download offline maps and confirm the next night’s lodging before leaving the area.

Day 3: Big Sur without a stopwatch

Drive the open portion of Highway 1 through Big Sur with no more than three priority stops. A viewpoint, a redwood walk and a relaxed lunch create a fuller day than repeated roadside braking. Pull over only in designated areas, never cross traffic for a photo and keep clear of fragile cliff edges. Mobile service is inconsistent, fuel is limited and businesses may close seasonally, so carry water and begin with a full tank.

Day 4: continue south or use the inland connection

Your exact route depends on verified road status. If Highway 1 is fully open, continue toward the Central Coast at a measured pace. If a closure interrupts through travel, follow official detours rather than map-app shortcuts on unsuitable roads. Build this day with the largest time buffer and book cancellable lodging when possible. The destination might be Cambria, Paso Robles or another practical stop that fits the legal, open route.

Day 5: Central Coast to Santa Barbara

Choose one character for the day: coastal towns, wine country or a beach-focused route. Trying to combine all three produces more parking and less experience. Reach Santa Barbara early enough to walk rather than immediately getting back in the car. The waterfront, downtown grid and nearby neighborhoods make a useful reset after several driving days. Check parking rules carefully and remove visible belongings whenever the vehicle is unattended.

Days 6 and 7: Malibu, Los Angeles and a calm finish

Use day six for the Santa Barbara-to-Los Angeles stretch, stopping selectively along the coast. Traffic can erase optimistic estimates, so avoid a fixed evening plan on the far side of the city. On day seven, return the car or explore one compact Los Angeles area instead of treating the whole region as a final-day checklist. Keep fuel, toll and rental-return requirements visible in your notes, and allow extra time around the airport.

What makes this route work

Reserve scarce coastal lodging, but keep daily stop lists flexible. Carry layers for fog and sun, download maps, inspect the vehicle each morning and share the route when traveling alone. Most importantly, verify official highway, park and weather information throughout the trip. The best Pacific Coast Highway drive is not the one that touches every named overlook. It is the one that stays safe, adapts quickly and gives the landscape enough time to become more than a background.
